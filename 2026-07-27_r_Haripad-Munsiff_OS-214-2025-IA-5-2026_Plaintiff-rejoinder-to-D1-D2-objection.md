    REJOINDER TO THE OBJECTION (OF 19.06.2026) BY THE DEFENDANT

1)  D2 (Ramesh Chandran, Managing Trustee) has sworn six times that he produced
     everything and concealed nothing:

     1.1) “nothing material has been concealed” — sworn thrice (IA-2/2025 & IA-
5/2025 replies, para 14; IA-9/2025 reply, para 10; all 16.02.2026).

     1.2) “[D2] has produced all documents” (D2’s objection to IA-5/2026 dated
19.06.2026 — para 7; and D2’s objection to IA-6/2026, paras 3 and 7).

    Having so sworn, D2 must not be allowed to approbate and reprobate any more;
    any document he now tenders in IA-5/2026 attracts the adverse inference under
     Section 119(g) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (“BSA”).

2)  D2 calls prayer (a) “infructuous” because IAs 2, 5 and 9 of 2025 were “disposed off”
    on 04.03.2026. That order merely “closed [them] in light of the affidavit” of
    16.02.2026 — yet D2’s own later productions (18.05.2026) falsify that affidavit:

     2.1) “nothing material has been concealed” [reply, para 14] — yet he held Lease
Deed dated 27.10.2025 (“the Lease”), registered 27.10.2025;

     2.2) “no assets/liabilities” 2007–2024 [reply, para 6] — yet the Trust’s Minutes
Book, p.11, records D2 himself depositing ₹1 crore to the “construction ac-
count” on 12.01.2024;

     2.3) “not received any funding… whatsoever” [reply, para 4] — yet the Minutes,
p.19, record Dr. Ravindran’s ₹1,00,00,000 (he died 27.08.2022).

     2.4) A closure on a false affidavit bars nothing (Sharada Sanghi v. Asha Agarwal,
       2026 INSC 292; S.P. Chengalvaraya Naidu v. Jagannath, (1994) 1 SCC 1).

     2.5) The High Court’s direction to dispose of the IAs (OP(C) 3278/2025, order
dated 15.01.2026) remains unfulfilled; prayer (a) is therefore live, and pray-
ers (b)–(c) unchallenged.
- 1 -

3)  On three matters D2 blows hot and cold:

     3.1) Beneficiary status — he pleads “the plaintiff is not a beneficiary” (the Objec-
tion, para 6), yet his Minutes (p.20) resolve to have the Plaintiff “terminated
from the beneficiary status through legal means”; one cannot terminate what
does not exist. D2 undisputedly knows the Plaintiff’s beneficiary status.

     3.2) Alterations — he pleads the lessee “shall not make any… alterations… of per-
manent nature” (the Objection, para 11), yet his Minutes (p.17) accept the
tenant’s request “to build a treatment block.”

     3.3) Suppression — he pleads the Plaintiff “suppressed [the lease]” (Objection,
para 3), yet the Lease names D2 “Presentant” and its Clause 21 keeps the
“Duplicate” with him. D2 does not explain how the Plaintiff conceals a deed
       D2 executed, presented, registered and holds.

4)  The lessee is not a “necessary party” (Objection, para 3).

     4.1) All reliefs are sought against the trustees alone, in personam; none against
the lessee.

     4.2) The Lease (27.10.2025) is a transfer pendente lite — the Meda and adjoining
property were already the subject of three pending suits when D2 leased it:

4.2.1) O.S. 84/2024 (06.03.2024) — the Plaintiff’s suit for a declaration of
his right and an injunction over dealings with the same property;

4.2.2) O.S. 243/2024 (14.08.2024) — D2’s own suit asserting the Trust’s
“absolute possession,” yet he sought no leave before leasing;

4.2.3) O.S. 214/2025 (12.06.2025) — the Plaintiff’s suit, in which D2 not
only concealed the Lease but also swore nothing was concealed
(head 1).

     4.3) A transferee pendente lite need not be joined and is bound regardless (Sec-
tion 52, Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (“TPA”); Order 1 Rule 9, Code of Civil
Procedure, 1908 (“CPC”); Sarvinder Singh v. Dalip Singh, (1996) 5 SCC 539;
Thomson Press v. Nanak Builders, (2013) 5 SCC 397).

     4.4) The lessee, incorporated only on 07.08.2025 — after the start of all three
suits — could not have been impleaded, because it was born pendente lite.

5)  Non-joinder of the trustees is no bar.


- 2 -

5.1) The reliefs run against the Trust (D1), on record through D2; non-joinder
defeats no petition (Order 1 Rule 9, CPC).

     5.2) Nor can D2 raise it: D2 individually and solely sued (O.S. 243/2024), de-
fended (O.S. 214/2025 and its IAs) and executed the Lease (27.10.2025).

     5.3) D2 signed the Lease on 27.10.2025. Trustees “ratified” only 89 days later, as
“the action taken by Managing Trustee” (Minutes, p.17). Signing before rati-
fication is itself proof that D2 acted alone, without authority, in breach of the
Indian Trusts Act, 1882 (“ITA”), s.48.

     5.4) Before 24.01.2026 ratification, D2 alone did the following for the lessee:
Gave the Trust’s NOC to seat the lessee’s registered office at the Meda (Ext.
H-1, 24.11.2025); gave D2’s own personal KSEB (electricity) bill to the lessee
as its proof of utility (Ext. H-2, 15.10.2025); bound the Trust to pay the
Meda’s property tax (Lease, Cl.11); reserved a 10-year renewal extending
the tie to twenty years (Lease, Cl.13(a)); wrote the Trust into a servient pos-
ture — no obstructing “the business of the Lessee,” no termination short of
three months’ default (Lease, Cll.12 and 14(1)); let the lessee register the
       Meda as its GST place of business ( 09.12.2025, Ext. H-5); and had his execu-
tion identified only by his wife, Jiji Ramesh (no other Trustee was involved).

6)   Misjoinder of D5–D7 is no bar: “no decree shall be reversed… on account of any
     misjoinder… of parties” (Section 99, CPC; Order 1 Rule 9, CPC). At most a name is
     struck because of misjoinder. Nothing is sought against D5–D7 in IA-5/2026.

7)  D2 pleads the triple tests favour him (Objection, para 12). They do not — because
     this Court has largely answered them already.

     7.1) Prima facie case.

7.1.1) Dismissing D2’s injunction over this property, this Court held the
Plaintiff would be “denuded from enjoying his right over plaint sched-
ule property” and the Trust “failed to establish a prima facie case” (the
03.12.2025 order in IA-1/2024 in O.S. 243/2024, paras 9–10). The
Lease also breaches the ITA throughout:

7.1.2) ITA s.48 (trustees must act jointly): D2 executed a 10-year lease
alone. The trustees “ratified” it 89 days later. Prima facie violation.

7.1.3) s.19 (duty to account): D2 admits keeping none; the sole rent credit is
₹60,000, on 24.04.2026; five other income agreements are withheld

- 3 -

by him to date. Prima facie violation of the duty to account.

     7.1.4) s.15 (trustee’s prudence): he leased 77% of the Trust land — despite
being authorised only to lease Meda for 10 years. With zero escala-
tion in rent for 10 years and zero security deposit. To an 81-day-old
company. Prima facie violation of duty to be prudent.

     7.1.5) s.36 (best rent): ₹60,000 flat for a 120-year-old Meda and 400-year-
old Nalukettu, with no competing quotes and no independent valua-
tion — at 0.45% annual return. Prima facie violation of best rent.

     7.1.6) s.17 (impartiality): he denies his nephew (the Plaintiff) as a benefi-
ciary, while committing ₹1.47 crore of the rent (total rent for over 20
years) to repay himself and his son (₹1,01,86,000, Minutes, p.19) and
his contractor (₹45 lakh at 7%, Minutes, p.18) as “debt owed,” on zero
accounts. Prima facie violation of impartiality to his brothers and
their children.

     7.1.7) s.14 (no adverse title): having failed in O.S. 243/2024 to oust the
Plaintiff as a “trespasser,” he installed a lessee to keep him out — a title
adverse to the beneficiary. Prima facie violation — Trustee assisted
adverse title set up by a third party against a beneficiary.

     7.1.8) s.11 (purpose): the Meda, a residential home since it was built in
1906 (Settlement Deed 299/I/2024), is being converted into a com-
mercial “treatment block.” The arch built by Kochu Kunju Channar
has already been torn down. Prima facie violation of the foundational
object of the Trust, “to preserve heritage” (Trust Deed 2/IV/2007, re-
cital of objects).

7.2) Irreparable injury — the harm is not compensable in money.

     7.2.1) Once public enters, the Trust’s pre-commercial financial and inven-
tory state is lost forever — the very loss this suit exists to prevent.

     7.2.2) The Meda (1906) and Nalukettu (1624) are recorded “residential”
(Settlement Deed 299/I/2024, Form 1B; LSGD register, Building IDs
50494010007872 / 50494010007874); conversion to a public well-
ness “treatment block” (Minutes, p.17) destroys the very character of
the home.

     7.2.3) Crores in antiques, heirlooms and furniture pass to the lessee before

- 4 -

any inventory (Minutes, pp.19–20). Two wildlife artifacts are already
missing (IA-9/2025), and D2’s only reply is “they don’t exist” — yet
D2 does not explain how he knows what exists and what does not, no
inventory of heirlooms having been made or produced to date.

7.2.4) The Plaintiff, aged 50, who lived his first 12 years in the Meda, is shut
from his ancestral home for next 10/20 years (Lease, Cll.2 and 13(a)).

7.2.5) The founding object — “preserving heritage of the family” (Trust Deed
2/IV/2007) — is inverted: the 400-year Alummoottil tharavad is
now marketed as “Sreemoolavasam Tharavad with 450 years herit-
age.” The family name is being erased from history (Exts. H-8 and H-
9).

7.2.6) D2 leased 29.42 Ares when only the Meda (plinth 4.645 Ares) was
authorised to be leased, for 10 years (Minutes, pp.16–17; Lease, p.8).

7.2.7) Maharwal Khewaji Trust v. Baldev Dass, (2004) 8 SCC 488, empha-
sized that pendente lite the nature of the property must be preserved.
This Lease changes and alienates the property from its beneficiaries.

7.2.8) If the court doesn’t step in now, both O.S. 84/2024 and O.S. 214/2025
before this court are rendered ineffective.

     7.3) Balance of convenience — this Court found “balance of convenience is not in
favour of” D1/D2 (order, 03.12.2025, para 10), and it has only tilted further.

7.3.1) The Plaintiff is a beneficiary by descent, assaulted and barred from his
home on 09.08.2024 and prosecuted (Crime 574/2024) by men D2
first swore were “employees… of the plaintiff trust” (O.S. 243/2024,
para 4) and now calls “no way connected” (the Objection, para 5).

7.3.2) D2 is a Trustee with zero accounts in 19 years. His “loss” is
₹60,000/month for property worth tens of crores — yet not a rupee
reaches the Trust for over 20 years; D2’s pocket swallows the rent as
₹1.47 crore of “debt,” created with zero accounts or GST invoices.

8)   Finally — why does D2 defend the tenant? His own records answer:

     8.1) The Lease starts 27.10.2025 (Lease, Cl.2); yet the Minutes fix commercial
terms only from 1.3.2026 (Minutes, p.17), and the first rent reached the
Trust’s account only on 24.04.2026 — so neither the Lease nor the resolu-
tion is being executed;

- 5 -

8.2) The NOC calls the Trust “the lawful owner” (Ext. H-1), yet the KSEB bill filed
to prove the premises stands in D2’s name personally (Ext. H-2) — how did
his personal bill make the Meda the tenant’s statutory address with the
       MCA?

     8.3) By the “ratification” of 24.01.2026 D2 had already done everything — Lease
signed (27.10.2025), NOC given (24.11.2025, Ext. H-1, concealed from
Court), the lessee’s office resolutions (Exts. H-3 and H-4), GSTIN at the Meda
(Ext. H-5), office shifted (Ext. H-6) — so the trustees “ratified” nothing.

     8.4) The AGOP + MOA + four ₹1,40,000/month rental agreements (Minutes,
pp.16–17) are completely missing in action; the only document produced is
the lease of the Meda and 29.42 Ares. The only rent to enter the Trust ac-
count is ₹60,000, six months late, on 24.04.2026.

For these reasons, D1/D2’s Objection (19.06.2026) raises no ground that answers IA-
5/2026. The Objection is unsustainable with no reason to withhold the reliefs sought.
