# Working skeleton: criminal complaint and linked civil recovery claims

**Matter:** Alummoottil Tharavad Trust / O.S. 214/2025

**Status:** Confidential working draft. This preserves every currently identified head of complaint. It is not a final pleading and must be settled against the original documents, applicable limitation periods, forum, and advice on jurisdiction before filing.

## 1. Purpose and non-waiver

This skeleton is designed to preserve the case against the people who attended the 24 January 2026 meeting, together with Bimal R. Madhavan and the contractor, M. C. Chandra Mohan / Chandra Mohanan B. It also preserves every presently identified financial, regulatory, document, and false-oath issue.

No issue is abandoned merely because the present bundle does not yet contain all supporting records. Where a fact still needs a source document, it is labelled **investigate and annex**, rather than deleted or overstated as already proved.

The intended filing structure is:

1. A criminal/court complaint that attaches the three earlier criminal complaints and their filing receipts, rather than presenting this as a fresh, unsupported police narrative.
2. A separate civil accounting, restitution, tracing, and personal-asset relief track.
3. A court-controlled route for any false affidavit or false evidence filed in pending litigation, including the procedure required for a Court complaint.
4. Tax, GST, FCRA, FEMA, and related regulatory referrals supported by a transaction schedule, not by a bare allegation.

## 4. Complaint heads - preserve all, plead only to the evidence available

### Head 1 - Criminal breach of trust and dishonest misappropriation

**Proposed legal route:** Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) section 316; alternatively section 314 where the facts establish dishonest misappropriation but the entrustment issue is contested.

**Primary allegation:** D2 had dominion over Trust money and records, accepted or caused the recording of Rs 11,99,85,900 as spent on Trust construction and land, and must account transaction by transaction for its receipt, custody, use, and benefit to the Trust.

**Persons:** D2 as the primary accused. Other attendees, signatories, account holders, recipients, and the contractor are to be included only where evidence proves knowing participation, knowing assistance, receipt, diversion, benefit, or wilful permission.

**Essential proof schedule:**

- every source of the Rs 11,99,85,900;
- every bank account and wallet used;
- every cash receipt/payment, date, payee, purpose, and voucher;
- land instrument, consideration, and title trail;
- contractor agreement, bills, work measurements, completion certificates, and GST details;
- proof that a claimed expenditure benefited the Trust rather than a private person;
- proof of individual knowledge/benefit for each non-D2 respondent.

**Civil consequence to preserve:** Seek a full account, restitution/surcharge, interest, tracing, and personal liability for the full admitted sum or such amount as forensic reconciliation proves. Do not concede a deduction merely because a later daybook repeats an unsupported expense.

### Head 2 - Abetment or criminal conspiracy

**Proposed legal route:** BNS sections 45, 49, and 61.

**Theory to preserve:** A meeting signature or attendance is not, by itself, a criminal offence. It becomes relevant only if the evidence shows that a person intentionally aided, instigated, concealed, agreed to, facilitated, received a benefit from, or knowingly ratified a dishonest transaction or false record.

**Particular inquiries:**

- Who wrote the 24 January minutes and who reviewed them before signing?
- Which attendee knew that the Trust account was opened only in May 2025 and had little recorded activity, yet approved or ratified the Rs 11,99,85,900 figure?
- Who arranged the contractor loan or negotiated its terms?
- Who prepared, forwarded, signed, received, or used the alleged NOC?
- Who benefited from repayment, rent, loan interest, construction contract, project-management role, or later transfer?

### Head 3 - False accounts, forged/false records, and false oath/evidence

**Proposed legal route:** BNS sections 227-229 (false evidence), sections 335-340 (false/forged document and use), and section 344 (falsification of accounts) only where its capacity and intent requirements are proved.

**Allegation to preserve:** Later-created daybooks, altered minutes, false signatures, and affidavits cannot replace contemporaneous accounts, invoices, vouchers, bank records, or original e-mails. If a document is fabricated, altered, antedated, falsely signed, or used knowing it to be false, plead its exact defect and the person responsible.

**Procedural safeguard:** If the alleged false oath/evidence concerns a document or affidavit used in a pending Court proceeding, seek the Court's complaint through the procedure in BNSS section 215. Do not frame that specific Court-evidence issue as if an independent private prosecution automatically bypasses the Court-complaint requirement.

**Future-events rider:** Every later oath, affidavit, daybook, invoice, or new production is preserved for a supplemental schedule. The schedule must record: statement; deponent; date; case/paragraph; contradicted by; source citation; materiality; and the procedural route.

### Head 4 - Income-tax / cash transaction referral

**Proposed legal route:** Income-tax Act, 1961 sections 269ST and 271DA; section 40A(3) where a deduction is claimed; and sections 194C/201 if contractor-payment/TDS duties apply.

**Allegation to preserve:** Investigate each alleged construction cash receipt of Rs 2,00,000 or more and each series of receipts from one person for one transaction/event. Section 269ST concerns the *recipient* of the specified cash receipt; not every cash payment automatically proves that contravention.

**Evidence needed before alleging evasion:**

- date, recipient, amount, mode, payer, and purpose;
- whether it was a single transaction/event or aggregated daily receipt;
- account-payee/electronic evidence or absence of it;
- relevant books, return, deduction claim, and TDS record;
- whether any statutory exception applies.

**Requested action:** Refer the completed schedule and supporting records to the Income-tax authorities for examination. Preserve the broader allegation of wilful tax evasion only if the records establish intentional concealment or a false return/claim.

### Head 5 - GST invoice, books, and evasion referral

**Proposed legal route:** CGST Act sections 31, 35, and 36; sections 122/132 only if the statutory elements are eventually established.

**Allegation to preserve:** The minutes acknowledge Rs 11,99,85,900 of construction and land expenditure, yet the present material contains no supporting GST invoice for the claimed construction expenditure. The complaint must request a GST investigation into the contractor/supplier chain, invoices, GSTINs, returns, e-way bills where applicable, work records, and payments.

**Accuracy safeguard:** Absence of a GST invoice in the current Court bundle is not alone proof of GST evasion. A tax invoice is required for taxable supplies by registered persons; some work or supply may require another lawful document or may be exempt. The final complaint should instead plead the precise, provable proposition: **no invoice/voucher has been produced for the stated expenditure, despite the duty to account**, and seek regulatory verification of whether taxable supplies, registration, invoice, return, or retention obligations were breached.

### Head 6 - Foreign funds: FCRA and FEMA examination

**Proposed legal route:** Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 (FCRA) sections 11, 17-20, and 35 if the statutory definition of foreign contribution/source and the Trust's status are satisfied; Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA) section 3 and applicable regulations only if the remittance/payment method or use involved a prohibited non-authorised transaction.

**Allegation to preserve:** Funds of a foreign national / person resident outside India were allegedly used, deposited into a personal account, or mingled with Trust corpus and then applied to construction without documented Trust authorization, FCRA registration/prior permission, or a compliant remittance/account trail.

**Essential verification before accusing a FEMA/FCRA offence:**

- identity, citizenship, residency/OCI status, and source of each contributor;
- whether the contributor was a "foreign source" under FCRA and whether any statutory exception applies;
- Trust program/status and any FCRA registration or prior permission;
- inward-remittance certificates, authorised-dealer bank records, purpose codes, FCRA account records, FC-4 filings, and utilization accounts;
- exact route from remittance to personal account, Trust corpus, contractor, or property.

**Requested action:** Preserve the complete FEMA/FCRA inquiry and referral. Do not label every lawful inward remittance as a contravention merely because the contributor lives abroad.

### Head 7 - Unauthorised NOC or forward to the lessee

**Allegation to preserve:** A lessee organization was allegedly given an NOC or authority without valid trustee authorization, by the signing/forwarding of a 21 August 2025 document.

**Current evidentiary position:** The available 21 August material gives D2 authority concerning lease/MOA documents after negotiation and legal vetting. It is not, by itself, the alleged NOC. The complaint must not merge the two documents.

**Investigate and annex:**

- original NOC and every page/annexure;
- exact author, signatory, sign date, recipient, and date received;
- exact 21 August forward/e-mail, its original metadata, attachment, and recipient;
- the precise authorization said to support the NOC;
- any legal-vetting record;
- lessee's incorporation, registered-office, GST, and filing records only to identify document use and timing;
- whether the NOC was used to obtain a statutory benefit, occupancy, registration, or business advantage.

**Possible legal consequence:** civil invalidity/unauthorised act, and BNS document offences only if the evidence establishes a false document, false authority, knowing use, intent to deceive, or another statutory element. The lessee remains a records custodian unless proof independently justifies a different role.

### Head 8 - Civil accounting, restoration, tracing, and personal asset protection

**Claims to preserve:**

- full account and production of original books, vouchers, invoices, bank accounts, contractor records, and land documents;
- surcharge/restitution for the full Rs 11,99,85,900 or the amount proved after forensic reconciliation;
- recovery from D2 and any other person whose knowing breach, joint act, receipt, benefit, or neglect enabled the loss;
- tracing of Trust money and any mixed fund/property; and
- interest, costs, and personal property relief proportionate to the proven claim.

**Legal framework to consider:** Indian Trusts Act sections 19, 23, 26, 27, 57, 63, and 66, subject to the Trust's legal character and the advice of counsel.

**Attachment/preservation limitation:** Any attachment before judgment must be supported by a precise affidavit identifying the personal property, value, and evidence that a defendant intends to obstruct or delay execution, as required by Order XXXVIII rule 5 CPC. It is not automatic from an accounting dispute. This skeleton does not seek a rent deposit, security, or any relief that treats the disputed lease as admitted or valid.

### Head 9 - Withheld documents and further false oaths

**Proceeding to preserve in O.S. 214/2025:** If a party states that records are unavailable, then later records, originals, accounts, metadata, invoices, or bank material emerge, append them to the withholding schedule. Seek the appropriate discovery/inspection order and, only after non-compliance with the applicable order, relief under Order XI rule 21 CPC. The rule is discretionary; it should be supported by the exact order, non-compliance, and prejudice.
