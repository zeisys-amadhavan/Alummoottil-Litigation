## Doctrine (maxims)

The negotiation maxims, with their legal equivalents — they carry over almost one-to-one:

| Maxim | Legal form it takes here |
|---|---|
| Existence ≠ disclosure | s.119 Ill.(g) BSA — a record admitted to exist but withheld is presumed unfavourable. |
| Exposed ≠ volunteered | Production compelled after demand earns no credit; the 18.05.2026 production cures nothing. |
| Reveal ≠ rewind | A later production cannot un-swear the 16.02.2026 oath; the falsity is fixed on the record. |
| Six months ≠ oversight | Duration of withholding is evidence of design, not accident — the "In existence since" column. |
| Denial ≠ forgetting | A sworn denial is a positive statement; its contradiction is perjury exposure, not lapse (s.215 BNSS). |
| Concealment ≠ consent | Silence about the lease/venture waives nothing; s.52 TPA binds the pendente-lite dealings. |
| Suppression ≠ status quo | Withholding does not preserve their position — each oath while suppressing adds a count. |
| Invoices ≠ ledgers | Demand third-party-anchored documents (GST invoices, live in the vendor's books and the GSTN portal), never party-authored ones (daybooks, minutes, their auditor's report) — the former cannot be faked, backdated or denied; the latter can be written tonight. |

## The objective: control

Control over the Trust as an institution — meaning control over (1) the **Trust** itself, (2) the **trustees as a body**, and (3) the **dependent beneficiaries** — such that the Trust is restructured on my terms.

Not "win a declaration." Not "remove one man." Restructure the whole arrangement.

## Why control, and why removal is not enough

Removing the Managing Trustee reaches **one** man. The objective is the **entire board** and the beneficiary class standing behind it. Any route that touches only D2 leaves the structure intact under the next trustee. The objective therefore requires an instrument that fixes **every trustee at once**.

## The legal strategy: Order XI Rule 21

The trustees defend O.S. 214/2025 **collectively**. That is the opening. A struck defence under **Order XI Rule 21**:

- fixes **all** of them, jointly, with the same on-record failure to account;
- collapses their shared "no connection / dormant / no accounts" position across **every** connected matter simultaneously;
- converts a contested suit into a won one — without their participation, on the strength of their own default.

That collective, judicially-recorded failure is the **foundation** everything else rests on. No trust-law remedy, no negotiation, and no single-defendant order reaches all trustees together. **Rule 21 is the one legal objective. Everything else is subsequent to it.**

**The gate now has a clock and a shape** (OP(C) 1864/2026, 14.07.2026): the Munsiff must pass a **merits** order on IA 6/2026 within two months of receipt — on her own reported estimate. Rule 21 is therefore a dated two-step: *(1)* an **itemized** production order (the Schedule — never a generic one), *(2)* a recorded partial default against it. A generic order is their escape; the Schedule is the trap.

**Named enemy counter-strategy — trickle production.** The 18.05 and 04.08 productions are not capitulation; they are Rule 21 insurance — partial compliance manufactured so any vague order can be answered with "produced." The counter is doctrine: specificity (the Schedule), completeness (s.42 BSA — no slice without the whole), and the five-oath record proving each production falsified the last.

**Proof-by-fee.** We do not retrieve what silence prices against them: a document's existence is proved by their own ledger (the ₹1,48,100 Sub-Registrar fee proves a registered instrument exists); non-production is theirs to explain and s.119(g) supplies the cost. No index-chasing.

**THE TRUMP CARD — the demand is the GST INVOICES behind ₹11,77,07,616: the 98% that never touched the produced bank account. Always. Never "accounts."** (Admitted spend ₹11,99,85,900 minus lifetime bank outflow ₹22,78,284.) The ~₹22 lakh of bank-visible vendor payments is the *specimen*, not the prize — its invoices prove this very project generated GST paper as routine, which pre-kills any claim that the remaining ₹11.77 crore of identical construction spending ran paperless. Party-authored records (daybook, minutes, their auditor's report) are cookable and serve one purpose only: the forensics/perjury case. The production demand, in every filing and every oral argument, is the invoice class — because invoices are *contemporaneous, third-party-anchored, and government-mirrored*:

- **Every invoice names both sides.** Rule 46 CGST Rules: supplier's name and GSTIN, and the recipient's name, address and GSTIN — and even for an unregistered recipient, any invoice above ₹50,000 must carry the recipient's name and delivery address. Every generator, every architect bill, every cement load names who bought it and where it went.
- **Every invoice exists in at least three places D2/D3 do not control**: the supplier's statutory duplicate (retained 72 months — s.36 CGST), the e-way bill portal for goods above ₹50,000 (consignor and consignee named), and the vendor's GSTR-1 filings on GSTN (flowing to GSTR-2A/2B for B2B).
- **The D3 excuse is dead twice over.** Mechanically: the recipient's copy may sit in Ohio, but the supplier's statutory copy sits in Kerala, the e-way bill sits in the portal, the return sits with the department — no permission, presence or cooperation of D3 is needed, ever. Factually: D3 is a US citizen resident in Ohio who did not manage the construction; **the person on the ground for every rupee was D2** — recipient of the contractor's 20.08.2025 estimate e-mail, claimant of ₹90,00,000 "Construction" in his own itemized list, chair of every minuted meeting. The duty to OBTAIN the vendor-side records is therefore D2's personally (ss.19, 47 ITA; his own "the managing trustee shall maintain the books" resolution; his admitted ground role). "Sivadasan won't furnish" (CA-31.07, ¶6) is not an answer — it is an admission that D2 never asked his own vendors for what the law obliges them to hand him.
- **PRODUCE OR REPAY — D2 alone, for the full amount. NO EMPTY CHAIR.** Section 23 ITA: every rupee of the ₹11,77,07,616 not evidenced by third-party paper — GST invoice, bill, e-way bill, or signed cash-payment voucher — is disallowed and pursued as surcharge against **D2 personally and solely**. His liability is primary, not vicarious: routing trust receipts through an unreachable co-trustee's US-linked personal account was D2's own choice — his imprudence (s.15), his accounting default (s.19), his concealment (s.26 exceptions). A managing trustee who deliberately parks trust money beyond the court's reach is charged as if it were in his own hands. **D3 is the conduit in the narrative, never the target of the recovery** — no relief is ever framed so that D2 can point to Ohio. Full repayment of ₹11,99,85,900 is the demand and the settlement lever; even the floor — unvouched sums disallowed, insider claims dead, personal-name assets restored — is catastrophic for D2, and s.316 CBT needs only a slice. (Decretal restitution only — never any interim deposit or security mechanism.)
- **THE CHARACTERIZATION DILEMMA — already decided by the situs of the record.** Personal money does not get minuted: nobody records private generosity in a trust's statutory minute book, itemizes it depositor-by-depositor to the rupee, resolves repayment schedules for it, and directs the Managing Trustee to compute EMIs on it. **The moment those figures entered the Trust's minutes over the trustees' signatures, they became trust receipts and trust liabilities** — ¶6's "personal assets" story died in their own book. If they nonetheless elect "personal" → the ₹1,01,86,000 repayment to D2/son, the ₹45-lakh 7% contractor loan, and every resolution servicing these "debts" from trust income are ultra vires and struck; the Trust owes nobody anything, ever. Either horn forfeits crores; the minutes make the trust-money horn the only one available. **Bonus casualty — dormancy:** Dr. Ravindran's ₹1,00,00,000 deposit predates his death on 27.08.2022, so by their own minutes crores flowed for trust construction from 2022 onward — while D2 swore on 16.02.2026 that the trust was "dormant." The deposits list is not only the quantum; it is another oath falsified by their own production.
- **The ultimatum, complete:** GST invoices and signed cash vouchers — or three cases: GST-evasion referral (row 37), s.316 BNS criminal breach of trust (rows 34–35), and the s.215 BNSS perjury reference (row 20). One trigger, three barrels, all pre-loaded.
- **The window has no pre-GST gap.** The renovation runs from the Dec 2022 resolution to 2026 — entirely inside the GST regime. Every vendor supply is invoice-mandated by s.31 CGST. The ₹54-lakh land answers to a registered deed — same doctrine, different registry.
- **The recipient-name field is the second trap.** When invoices surface, the named buyer decides the case's character: the Trust as recipient → Trust expenditure, accounts owed, s.19 ITA in full; Sivadasan or the contractor as recipient → ₹11.45 crore of "Trust renovation" privately invoiced — misappropriation in documentary form.
- **The fork has no safe exit — and the specimen slams the denial branch.** Produce → ₹11.77 crore becomes traceable, vendors summonable, the recipient test runs. Deny existence → a sworn claim that the same site, same period and same trades that generated invoices for 2% of the money (Powermech, Heavens, Pittappillil — on their own bank statement) ran paperless for the other 98% — an unbilled-cash confession opening the GST-intelligence and Income-Tax track at zero cost to us.
- **The payer trail runs through D3’s personal account** — so the vendor-side records (supplier copies, e-way bills, GSTR-1) map who paid and who took delivery at the Meda site, from a direction D2 never controlled: the hidden 98% reconstructed without a single document of theirs.
- **We verify independently and in parallel** — never waiting on their production: s.91 BNSS summons to the jurisdictional GST officers and GSTN for the vendors' filings and e-way bills naming the Trust, the trustees or the site address; and to the vendors directly (Powermech, Pittappillil, Heavens Architects, the contractor) for their sale-invoice copies to the Alummoottil project 2022–2026. The truth arrives from the government and the counterparties — cookproof.

**Language discipline:** in pleadings, arguments, and this file, the ask is "the GST invoices, bills, e-way bills and signed cash-payment vouchers evidencing the ₹11,77,07,616 of admitted expenditure not reflected in the produced bank account" (the ₹22 lakh bank-visible invoices sought separately as a completeness item — never allowed to masquerade as compliance) — the phrase "produce the accounts" is retired except where s.19 ITA's *rendition* relief is pleaded as the legal peg. Books are what they write; invoices are what happened.

## The secondary legal lever: criminal FIR registration

Once the failure to account is judicially fixed, criminal exposure opens against the trustees **as a body** — not as individuals to be picked off, but collectively:

- **breach of trust on common intention** — every trustee who sanctioned the collective "[D3] need not account" resolution;
- **tacit approval of the physical attack** on the beneficiary;
- **false sworn statements** — a reference under s.215 BNSS for the perjured "dormant / no accounts" stand.

Criminal registration is what converts a civil win into real-world control. Two tracks, sequenced differently:

- **Assault track — live and independent (not gated).** FIR 956/2024 and CMP 2828/2024 predate everything and are continuously grounded. Now corroborated by the **paymaster evidence**: the Defendants' own daybook records Trust cash to D5 (Arun B. Unni) in March 2026 — after D5 swore "no connection" (18.02.2026) and before D1–D2 swore he worked "much earlier" (19.06.2026). The FIR-complainant on the Defendants' payroll, by the Defendants' own ledger. Pressed toward **grievous hurt with common intention** — non-bailable exposure for D2, D3 and D5–D7.
- **CBT track — ₹11,99,85,900 criminal breach of trust (s.316 BNS; cognizable, non-bailable) — gated on the Rule 21 window.** Entrustment (Deed 2/IV/2007 trusteeship) + dominion (₹11.99 crore admittedly routed through D3's personal account and the undisclosed "Construction account") + conversion indicators (1.9% traceable; insider repayment resolutions; trust funds paying the trustees' defence). Files within days of the recorded day-16 default, citing the merits order and the default — so no police officer can park it as "purely civil." Complainant of record: A.G. Kishore (POA, India-resident); conducted by Thaha; Anoop offered by video conference in the complaint itself to pre-empt "unable to reach." Route: SHO → SP → Magistrate u/s 175(3) BNSS with the Priyanka Srivastava affidavit. Early-trigger exception: if the produced ₹54-lakh land deed shows the land in a trustee's personal name, file immediately.
- **Perjury track — gated on the IA 6/2026 merits order.** The s.215 BNSS reference (five oaths) files only after the court's own order fixes the withholding; filed early it is "a party's grievance," filed after it is "the court's record."

## How control is actually realized

By **leverage**, not by adjudication. Rule 21 plus criminal exposure makes the trustees' position untenable and makes the dependent beneficiaries' interests unsafe to keep defending. Control is then taken **by consent** — releases, restructuring, resignations — because the alternative on the table is ruin. The decree is the threat; the settlement is the payoff.
