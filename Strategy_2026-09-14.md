### S1c — Enforcement / Rule 21

Rule 21 remains an internal, later-stage remedy. Do not invoke it until the record shows:

1. clear operative discovery/production obligation;
2. defendant identified;
3. proved service or knowledge;
4. definite compliance period;
5. expiry;
6. itemized non-compliance;
7. any final opportunity required by fairness / precedent;
8. continued wilful default;
9. prejudice.

## ₹11.99 crore focus

The Trust's own minutes record **₹11,99,85,900** as expenditure. The compliance question is not merely whether an account total is produced; it is whether the defendants disclose and produce the source material within their possession, custody or power that supports the admitted figure.

For every component, test:

**amount → alleged payer → alleged payee → date → invoice/bill → payment proof → ledger entry → GST/tax treatment → produced/not produced → sworn explanation for absence.**

If a source record never existed or is no longer held, require the sworn discovery to state the legally required particulars rather than accepting a generic “not available.”

## Third-party records

## Criminal track

Civil non-compliance materially strengthens the factual record of concealment only when read with the surrounding evidence. It does **not** by itself prove criminal breach of trust, conversion or dishonest intent.

Criminal use remains evidence-led:
- entrustment / dominion;
- authorized purpose;
- specific conversion/use/disposal evidence;
- responsible person;
- loss;
- knowledge / intent where legally required.
